    Ten interview questions for any AI hire

    They tell people who build with AI apart from people who only talk about it.

    1. Walk me through how you tested your last AI feature before it shipped.

      Strong answer: a named set of test cases, a metric, and failures they found and fixed. Red flag: “It looked right in the demo.”

    2. What did your last AI workflow cost to run per month, and how do you know?

      Strong answer: a cost per run, deliberate model choices and spending caps. Red flag: no idea, or “the company paid for it.”

    3. Where does a human approve what the AI does, and why there?

      Strong answer: approval steps placed by risk, such as money, customers and actions that cannot be undone. Red flag: either everything is automatic or nothing is.

    4. Tell me about an AI output that was wrong in production. How did you find out?

      Strong answer: monitoring or checks caught it, and a test was added afterwards. Red flag: a customer found it, and nothing changed.

    5. When would you not use an LLM for this problem?

      Strong answer: when rules, search or a spreadsheet is cheaper and more reliable. Red flag: AI for everything.

    6. How do you choose between better prompts, retrieval (RAG) and fine-tuning?

      Strong answer: the cheapest option first, moving up only when the data says so. Red flag: jumping straight to fine-tuning.

    7. How do you keep API keys and customer data out of prompts, logs and code?

      Strong answer: a secrets manager, redaction and least-privilege access. Red flag: keys pasted into the code “just for now.”

    8. What did you research before writing the first prompt on your last project?

      Strong answer: the users, the data, the constraints and what “good” looks like. Red flag: they started prompting on day one.

    9. Show me something you built that other people use. What did they complain about?

      Strong answer: real users, specific complaints and visible changes in response. Red flag: only demos and screenshots.

    10. How would you prove this AI feature made the business better?

      Strong answer: a baseline and a business metric, such as hours saved, conversion or cost. Red flag: “People engaged with the AI.”
